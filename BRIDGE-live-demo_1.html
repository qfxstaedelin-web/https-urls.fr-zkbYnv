About BRIDGE

Made by students, for students

“Pick a side. Then hear the other one.”

Institutional app · HSG

A new topic every week

Every week the student editorial team proposes one new topic, with a fresh briefing and new matches. Topics start with fundamental concepts and get more complex as you progress.

Who runs it

A student organisation

BRIDGE is run by HSG students who are dedicated to innovating in global communication. Because it is an institutionally born app, it is built for HSG students and provided by them.

Students decide the topics and write the briefings. Selected teachers assist them in consolidating information and act as quality controllers, so that what you read is factually valid.

Where the information comes from

Pluralism, not one newsroom

Each briefing integrates diverse journalistic sources so that the picture stays balanced. You can also ask where exactly a sentence came from: every claim is traceable back to the article behind it.

How a session works
  1. PositionReact to short statements from strongly disagree to strongly agree.
  2. MatchYou are paired with a student who sees it differently. You can decline.
  3. PrepareBoth of you get the same briefing, with facts and opinion kept apart.
  4. DebateA private one-to-one chat. No followers, no likes, nothing to go viral.
  5. Reflect“What was the strongest argument you heard from the other side?”
  6. ProgressConstructive debates unlock more sensitive topics and earn campus rewards.

The AI evaluates how people communicate, not what they believe. An unpopular opinion never lowers your score by itself.

What comes next

If the HSG pilot works, BRIDGE could open to partner universities across the world — bringing many more students, languages and ideas into the same conversations.

This week's topic

Should Switzerland raise the retirement age to 67?

Difficulty 2 / 4 412 students positioned Pensions · Generations

You will be asked to position yourself on: “Switzerland should raise the statutory retirement age to 67.”

Established facts

The statutory reference age is 65 for women and men, after the AHV 21 reform raised women's reference age in three-month steps. Life expectancy at 65 has risen substantially since 1970, while the number of contributors per pensioner has fallen.

Contested interpretations

How large the financing gap actually is depends on assumptions about migration, wage growth and investment returns — economists disagree on all three. It is also disputed whether a higher reference age genuinely saves money or partly shifts costs to unemployment and disability insurance.

Where opinion begins

How the burden should be shared between generations, and whether physically demanding jobs justify a differentiated retirement age, are political choices. Neither follows from the figures above.

NZZCentre-right daily4 Sep 2026

Wie viel Reform die AHV wirklich braucht

The paper argues that the AHV's demographic problem is structural rather than cyclical: the ratio of contributors to pensioners keeps falling, and a later reference age is the lever that acts on both sides of the equation at once.

It concedes that the size of the gap is model-dependent, and that recent revenue has repeatedly come in better than forecast — which critics read as a reason to wait.

Its conclusion is that indexing the reference age to life expectancy would take the question out of four-year political cycles, but that this only works if physically demanding occupations get a separate route.

SRF NewsPublic broadcaster5 Sep 2026

Die Zahlen hinter der Rentendebatte

An explainer that separates what is measured from what is projected. It shows how the AHV's yearly result has moved, and how much of the long-run deficit depends on the assumed migration path.

Two economists are given equal space: one treats a later reference age as unavoidable arithmetic, the other points out that productivity growth and higher labour-force participation could close much of the gap without it.

The piece ends on the political reality that Swiss voters have rejected several pension reforms and accepted others by narrow margins.

Le TempsCentre-left daily3 Sep 2026

Retraite à 67 ans: le débat que la Suisse romande n'a pas voulu

Reports from the French-speaking cantons, where opposition to a higher reference age is strongest, and links it to occupational structure: manual and care work is over-represented, and the years of healthy life after 65 are not evenly distributed.

Union representatives argue that raising the age is a wage cut in disguise for people who cannot keep working. Employer associations answer that flexible retirement already exists and is under-used.

The article notes that both camps cite the same federal statistics and reach opposite conclusions — which is precisely the disagreement BRIDGE asks students to hold open.

The EconomistLiberal weekly30 Aug 2026

Europe's slow creep towards working longer

Places Switzerland in a European comparison, where most countries have already legislated a reference age of 67 or tied it to life expectancy — and where the political cost of doing so has been high everywhere.

The comparison cuts both ways: countries that raised the age saw pension spending stabilise, but also saw a rise in disability and long-term unemployment among older workers.

Included in the briefing to show students that the same reform has produced different results depending on the labour market it landed in.

What to try

Design decisions

Private, not public
No followers, no likes, nothing to go viral — this avoids giving polarising “pushers” a bigger stage (Brandsma's framework).
Matching is not our invention
My Country Talks already shows the model works. BRIDGE adds the briefing, the mediator, staged difficulty, reflection and campus rewards.
Communication, not belief
The score reacts to insults and refusal to engage, never to the position held. Criteria are public and decisions can be appealed.
Faculty as quality control
Students write the briefings; selected teachers sign off on factual validity, and every claim stays traceable to its article.
